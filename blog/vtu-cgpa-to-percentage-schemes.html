VTU CGPA to Percentage: 2018 vs 2021 Scheme Formulas

VTU is the university most students get wrong, because the formula genuinely changed between schemes. Use the wrong one and your "percentage" can be off by 7.5 points.

The two formulas

  • 2015 / 2017 / 2018 schemes: Percentage = (CGPA − 0.75) × 10. This is the official formula in VTU's OBE/CBCS regulations (18OB §7.1): "Percentage of marks secured, P = (CGPA earned − 0.75) × 10."
  • 2021 scheme onwards: Percentage = CGPA × 10. Multiple VTU-focused calculators and guides report that the 2021, 2022 and 2025 schemes dropped the 0.75 adjustment — but VTU's own circular for this change is harder to pin down, so treat it as "reported, verify."

For an 8.5 CGPA, that's the difference between 77.5% (2018 scheme) and 85% (2021 scheme). Same university, same CGPA, wildly different percentage — which is exactly why our calculator lists VTU twice and asks for your scheme.

Worked examples

2018 scheme: 7.5 CGPA

  1. Subtract 0.75: 7.5 − 0.75 = 6.75
  2. Multiply by 10: 6.75 × 10 = 67.5
  3. Result: 67.5%

2021 scheme: 8.2 CGPA

  1. Multiply by 10: 8.2 × 10 = 82
  2. Result: 82%

How VTU grading feeds the CGPA

VTU uses a 10-point CBCS scale. The published grade mapping is: 90–100 → O (10), 80–89 → A+ (9), 70–79 → A (8), 60–69 → B+ (7), 55–59 → B (6), 50–54 → C (5), 40–49 → P (4), below 40 → F (0). Your SGPA for a semester is Σ(credits × grade points) ÷ Σ(credits); your CGPA is the credit-weighted average of semester SGPAs. Confirm the bands against your scheme handbook, since grade boundaries are the other thing universities occasionally revise.

Which scheme are you on?

Your scheme is determined by your year of admission, and it's printed on your grade cards. Students admitted in 2018–2020 are almost certainly on the 2018 scheme; 2021 admissions onwards fall under the 2021 scheme. If you're unsure, your college exam cell can confirm in seconds — and while you're there, ask them to confirm the conversion formula too, since that settles the "reported ×10" question definitively for your batch.

The placement warning

This is the practical reason the scheme distinction matters: a 2018-scheme student with 8.5 CGPA who reports "85%" (the 2021 formula, or a generic calculator's output) is overstating their official percentage by 7.5 points. If a recruiter cross-checks against VTU's formula during background verification, the discrepancy looks like misrepresentation — and offer revocations over exactly this have been reported. Always convert with your scheme's formula, and keep the regulation reference with your application documents.

What changed in 2021 — and why alumni should care

The reported switch to ×10 for the 2021 scheme onward creates a subtle trap for alumni. If you graduated under the 2018 scheme, your official percentage is fixed by the formula in force during your degree — it does not update because the university later changed the rule. Your 2019 transcript reading 8.5 CGPA means 77.5% forever, even if a 2023 graduate with the same CGPA legitimately reports 85%. When a verification agency checks your credentials, they apply the regulation of your batch. Alumni re-computing their percentage with "the new formula" to look better on LinkedIn are manufacturing exactly the kind of discrepancy that gets flagged.

The reverse trap catches current students: using an old calculator (or a senior's advice) that still applies (CGPA − 0.75) × 10 to a 2021-scheme CGPA understates your percentage by 7.5 points — potentially dropping you below a placement cutoff you actually clear. When in doubt, the scheme printed on your grade card settles it.

Common VTU-specific mistakes

  1. Using CBSE's ×9.5. The single most common error. VTU's formula exists precisely because ×9.5 doesn't fit VTU's grade distribution — using it inflates a 2018-scheme 8.5 CGPA from 77.5% to 80.75%.
  2. Averaging SGPAs instead of weighting. VTU semesters carry different credit loads (typically 20–25). A straight average of eight SGPAs is not your CGPA; the credit-weighted average is. The difference is small in most cases but it's the kind of small that matters at a 60% cutoff.
  3. Mixing scheme formulas mid-degree. Lateral-entry and scheme-change students sometimes have semesters under different regulations. Your CGPA follows your admitted scheme's rules end to end — confirm with the exam cell rather than blending formulas.

Where to find the official circular

VTU publishes its regulations at vtu.ac.in under the examinations/regulations section. The 2018-scheme formula lives in the "Regulations Governing the Degree of Bachelor of Engineering/Technology under Outcome Based Education and Choice Based Credit System (CBCS), effective 2018–19" — section 18OB §7.1, which states the conversion as P = (CGPA − 0.75) × 10. For the 2021 scheme, look for the corresponding 2021-scheme regulation document and check its conversion section directly; if the document is ambiguous, a written confirmation from your college exam cell (an email suffices) is the practical gold standard. Bookmark the regulation PDF once you find it — you'll need the same reference at every placement and application deadline, and having it ready beats scrambling for it later.
