How to Calculate SGPA from Marks and Credits (Step by Step)

SGPA looks intimidating until you see it done once. It's three steps — marks to grade points, weight by credits, divide — and then the same idea scales up to CGPA.

Step 1: Convert each subject's marks to grade points

Your university maps marks ranges to grade points on a 10-point scale. A typical CBCS mapping (confirm against your handbook) looks like this:

  • 90–100 → 10 grade points
  • 80–89 → 9
  • 70–79 → 8
  • 60–69 → 7
  • 50–59 → 6 (some universities split 55–59 → 6, 50–54 → 5)
  • 40–49 → 4 or 5 (the pass grade — varies)
  • Below 40 → 0 (fail)

The exact bands differ by university and regulation, so always use yours. The method below works regardless.

Step 2: Weight by credits and add up

For each subject, multiply its credits by its grade points. Add all those products. Then divide by the semester's total credits:

SGPA = Σ (subject credits × grade points) ÷ Σ (subject credits)

Worked example

Say your semester looked like this:

  • Data Structures — 4 credits, 78 marks → 8 grade points → 4 × 8 = 32
  • Operating Systems — 4 credits, 85 marks → 9 grade points → 4 × 9 = 36
  • DBMS Lab — 2 credits, 92 marks → 10 grade points → 2 × 10 = 20
  • Mathematics — 3 credits, 64 marks → 7 grade points → 3 × 7 = 21
  • Elective — 3 credits, 71 marks → 8 grade points → 3 × 8 = 24

Total credit-points: 32 + 36 + 20 + 21 + 24 = 133. Total credits: 4 + 4 + 2 + 3 + 3 = 16.

SGPA = 133 ÷ 16 = 8.31

Notice how the 2-credit lab barely moves the needle while the 4-credit theory subjects dominate — that's the weighting doing its job.

Step 3: From SGPA to CGPA

CGPA applies the same weighting one level up: each semester's SGPA weighted by that semester's total credits.

CGPA = Σ (semester SGPA × semester credits) ÷ Σ (all semester credits)

Example: Sem 1 SGPA 8.31 (16 credits), Sem 2 SGPA 7.90 (20 credits), Sem 3 SGPA 8.60 (22 credits):

(8.31 × 16) + (7.90 × 20) + (8.60 × 22) = 132.96 + 158.00 + 189.20 = 480.16; total credits = 58.

CGPA = 480.16 ÷ 58 = 8.28

Three mistakes that silently wreck the maths

  1. Averaging SGPAs directly. (8.31 + 7.90 + 8.60) ÷ 3 = 8.27 — close here, but with unequal semester credits the error grows. Always weight by credits.
  2. Using the wrong grade-point bands. A 79 that maps to 8 at your university might map to 7 at another's. One band off on a 4-credit subject swings the SGPA noticeably.
  3. Forgetting backlogs. Failed subjects (0 grade points) still count their credits in the denominator until cleared — that's why one backlog dents the SGPA so hard. Re-check your university's rule on how reappear grades are treated.

Grade bands differ more than you'd think

The example above used one common mapping, but universities tune their bands. VTU's published CBCS mapping runs 90–100 → 10 down to 40–49 → 4 (pass), while Anna University's runs 91–100 → 10 with different intermediate cutoffs, and some universities award 0 below 50 rather than below 40. A single marks difference at a band boundary — 79 vs 80, say — can shift a subject's grade points by a full point, which on a 4-credit subject moves the SGPA by 0.25. Two practical consequences: first, always compute with your university's band table, not a generic one; second, if you're ever disputing a grade, the band boundaries are where the leverage is.

Labs, electives, and audit courses

Not every course counts the same way. Laboratory courses usually carry fewer credits (1–2) but are graded on the same 10-point scale, so they enter the sum normally — just with less weight. Electives count fully at their stated credits. Audit / non-credit courses (often marked PP/NP or S/U) typically carry zero credits and are excluded from the SGPA entirely — but confirm this, because a few universities include them. Backlog subjects you've registered for but not cleared contribute 0 grade points while their credits sit in the denominator, which is why a single backlog can drag an otherwise strong SGPA down by half a point or more.

Planning ahead: what SGPA do you need next semester?

The formula works backwards too, and this is genuinely useful for target-setting. Rearranged:

Required SGPA = (target CGPA × total credits after next sem − earned credit-points so far) ÷ next sem's credits

Example: after 3 semesters you have 58 credits and 480.16 credit-points (CGPA 8.28). You want to finish semester 4 (22 credits) at 8.50 overall. Total credits become 80; you need 8.50 × 80 = 680 credit-points; you have 480.16, so you need 199.84 more from 22 credits → required SGPA = 9.08. That's the difference between "I need to do well" and "I need 9.08" — the second one tells you exactly how much slack you have per subject.

Worked example: what a backlog does to your SGPA

Take the semester from the worked example (SGPA 8.31 across 16 credits), but suppose the 3-credit Mathematics subject was a backlog — 0 grade points, credits still counted:

  • Credit-points become 32 + 36 + 20 + 0 + 24 = 112 (instead of 133)
  • Total credits stay 16
  • SGPA = 112 ÷ 16 = 7.00 (instead of 8.31)

One failed 3-credit subject cost 1.31 SGPA points — nearly a full grade band. That's the denominator effect: the credits don't disappear just because the grade points did. Clear backlogs early not just for the degree requirement, but because every uncleared semester keeps taxing your CGPA.
